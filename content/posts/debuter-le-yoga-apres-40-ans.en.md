"I'm not flexible enough for yoga." It is the sentence we hear most often on the phone, and almost always from people over forty. Good news: flexibility is not a prerequisite, it is a result. Here is our advice for walking into your first class without worrying.

## Pick the right class

To begin, choose a **gentle hatha** or an **all-levels hatha** class. Postures are held longer and explained step by step, and the teacher has time to correct you. Vinyasa can come later, once the basic postures feel familiar. If you have chronic pain, a joint replacement or a specific condition, tell us before class: we will adapt.

## Come as you are

No technical outfit needed: soft trousers and a T-shirt are enough. We practise barefoot. Just avoid eating in the two hours before. Mats, blocks and straps are provided at the studio, and props are not a "beginner's crutch": our teachers use them every day.

## Listen to your body, not your neighbour

Yoga is not a competition. In the same class you will see straight legs and bent knees, hands on the floor and hands on a block. What matters is the feeling of stretch, never pain. A simple rule: if you can no longer breathe calmly through your nose, you have gone too far.

## Breathing, your best ally

At first, linking breath and movement feels complicated. Don't aim for perfection: just don't hold your breath. Breathe in as you open, breathe out as you fold or come down. After a few sessions it becomes natural, and that is often when you feel the first effects on stress and sleep.

## Consistency before intensity

One class a week for three months brings far more than an intensive weekend with no follow-up. The body needs time to loosen up, and regular practice builds a habit. Many of our students start with a 5-class pass, then switch to the weekly membership once the Tuesday evening class has become their appointment with themselves.

## And after class?

Take five minutes before you leave. A herbal tea is waiting in the lounge, and it is the right moment to ask your questions. That night you will probably sleep better; the next day you may feel a little sore, but it fades quickly.
