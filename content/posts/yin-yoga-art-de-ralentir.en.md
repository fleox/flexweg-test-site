When the days get shorter and the diary fills up, the body asks for something other than intensity. It is the perfect season to discover **yin yoga**, a slow practice that works deep down.

## What is yin yoga?

Born in the United States in the late 1970s and inspired by Chinese medicine, yin yoga offers floor postures, mostly seated or lying down, held for **three to five minutes**. Where hatha and vinyasa work the muscles (the "yang"), yin targets connective tissue: fascia, ligaments, joints. These tissues only loosen with gentle, sustained traction.

## Three simple principles

1. **Find your edge**: move into the posture until you feel a clear but comfortable sensation, never more.
2. **Stay still**: once settled, relax the muscles and stop moving, unless discomfort appears.
3. **Hold over time**: duration does the work. The minutes feel long at first, then the mind settles.

## Who is it for?

Everyone, and especially athletes (runners, cyclists, climbers) whose hips and lower back are often stiff, stressed people who find it hard to switch off, and anyone who sleeps badly. Yin is also a great complement to a dynamic practice such as vinyasa: two sessions a week, one yang and one yin, make an ideal balance.

## What happens in class

Inès starts with a few minutes of breathing, then offers five or six postures: caterpillar, butterfly, dragon, half saddle, reclined twist. Cushions, blankets and blocks let everyone find comfortable support. Class ends with a long savasana, and on Sunday evenings with a Tibetan singing bowl sound bath.
